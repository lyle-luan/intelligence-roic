# Research Process

> I-ROIC must preserve reasoning, not just conclusions.

## 1. Research modes

Every artifact should primarily belong to one of four modes.

### Theory

A current model of how the system works.

Location: `docs/` and `frameworks/`.

### Company / system research

Evidence about a participant, infrastructure layer or economic mechanism.

Location: `research/`.

### Research log

Chronological record of how the theory changed.

Location: `notes/research-log/`.

### Case

A concrete historical or operational example used to test the framework.

Location: `cases/`.

## 2. Claim discipline

Important claims should be tagged mentally or explicitly as:

- **Question**
- **Hypothesis**
- **Observation**
- **Inference**
- **Current conclusion**

Do not silently promote a hypothesis into a conclusion.

## 3. Preserve the causal chain

For every major thesis change, record:

1. What did we believe before?
2. What observation or question challenged it?
3. What alternative explanation appeared?
4. What changed in the model?
5. What evidence would falsify the new view?

The repository should allow a future reader to reconstruct the evolution of I-ROIC.

## 4. Company research sequence

For each company:

1. Identify the intelligence it can access or produce.
2. Map its Intelligence Sinks.
3. Estimate the value density of each sink.
4. Identify action/validation constraints.
5. Estimate capture rate.
6. Map proprietary feedback loops.
7. Estimate all-in intelligence cost.
8. Separate average from marginal return.
9. Estimate reinvestment runway.
10. Translate the result into cash flow and valuation.

Do not start from "AI company" labels.

## 5. Evidence hierarchy

Prefer, roughly:

1. audited financial disclosures;
2. official operating metrics and technical disclosures;
3. customer / user evidence;
4. direct product behavior;
5. credible technical analysis;
6. management claims;
7. market narratives.

Management statements are inputs, not proof.

## 6. Avoid common traps

### Token-volume fallacy

More tokens consumed does not prove more value created.

### Benchmark fallacy

A better benchmark score does not prove a better business.

### Revenue-only fallacy

AI revenue can understate value when intelligence is consumed internally.

### Cost-only fallacy

Lower inference cost does not guarantee high economic return.

### Data-moat fallacy

Large datasets can be stale, replicable, legally unusable or weakly connected to action.

### Automation fallacy

Replacing human steps can create savings but also increase validation and failure costs.

### Double-counting

Do not count both the AI productivity gain and the same gain again as a separate terminal-value uplift.

## 7. Versioning the theory

When a core definition or causal mechanism changes:

- update the canonical theory file;
- add a dated research log explaining why;
- preserve the old reasoning in history rather than rewriting the past.

## 8. Research output standard

A mature company report should end with:

- thesis;
- sink map;
- value-capture map;
- feedback-loop map;
- cost/capital map;
- I-ROIC evidence;
- marginal return;
- reinvestment runway;
- valuation implications;
- disconfirming evidence;
- confidence level;
- next research questions.
