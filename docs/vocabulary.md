# Vocabulary

> Canonical working terms. Definitions are provisional and should be revised when the model changes.

## Intelligence

Machine-produced capability used for reasoning, generation, prediction, planning, coding, decision support or autonomous action.

The term is deliberately broader than a specific model family.

## Intelligence Input

The usable unit of machine intelligence consumed by a workflow.

Tokens are one implementation-level measurement, not the economic unit itself.

## Intelligence Seller

An actor whose primary monetization path is selling access to intelligence through APIs, subscriptions, licenses or hosted inference.

## Intelligence Allocator

An actor that decides where intelligence should be deployed to maximize economic return.

An allocator may also be a producer or seller.

## Internal Intelligence Consumption

Use of machine intelligence inside the owner’s own research, product, operations, sales, software development, physical systems or capital allocation rather than sale to an external customer.

## Intelligence Sink

A destination where intelligence can be turned into an economically meaningful action or outcome.

Examples can include software workflows, research processes, customer interactions, factories, vehicles, devices, transaction systems and investment decisions.

## Sink Quality

The ability of an Intelligence Sink to absorb additional intelligence at attractive marginal returns.

## Capture Rate

The fraction of gross economic value created by intelligence that is retained by the firm.

## Feedback Loop

A mechanism by which using intelligence generates proprietary observations, memory, data, workflow changes or outcomes that improve future intelligence deployment.

## Memory

Persistent context that changes future decisions.

Memory can exist at user, workflow, organization, product, physical-system or market level.

Memory is not assumed to be a moat. It becomes strategically important only when it is valuable, persistent, difficult to replicate, and connected to action.

## Agency

The ability of intelligence to take or cause repeated actions rather than only provide answers.

## Intelligence Production Stack

The physical and software stack that turns energy and capital into usable machine intelligence.

## Intelligence Execution Stack

The systems that turn a model output into validated action.

## Economic I-ROIC

Incremental after-tax operating profit attributable to intelligence divided by incremental invested capital required to create and execute it.

## Intelligence Productivity

A unit-economic proxy measuring economic value created or cost avoided per all-in intelligence execution cost.

## Marginal I-ROIC

Return on the next unit of intelligence-related capital deployed.

## Personal I-ROIC

The personal analogue: value produced by intelligence under an individual’s control relative to the person’s scarce inputs — attention, time, capital, compute and decision capacity.

## Personal Capital Stack

The durable assets that allow an individual to compound intelligence: memory, capital, distribution, relationships, owned businesses and agent organization.
