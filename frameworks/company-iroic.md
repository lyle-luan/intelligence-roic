# Company I-ROIC Framework

Use this template for company-level research.

## 1. Company role

Classify the company by economic role, not marketing label.

Possible roles:

- intelligence producer;
- intelligence seller;
- intelligence allocator;
- compute / infrastructure provider;
- digital sink owner;
- physical sink owner;
- integrated stack owner.

A company may occupy multiple roles.

## 2. Intelligence source

- Own model?
- Third-party model?
- Open-source model?
- Mixed routing?
- Unique cost advantage?
- Unique capability advantage?
- How durable is that advantage?

## 4. All-in cost map

Include:

- model;
- inference;
- energy;
- context / retrieval;
- memory;
- orchestration;
- human oversight;
- validation;
- rework;
- execution infrastructure.

## 5. Value creation

Measure where possible:

- revenue uplift;
- conversion improvement;
- churn reduction;
- labor saving;
- cycle-time reduction;
- R&D acceleration;
- software throughput;
- physical utilization;
- yield / quality improvement;
- asset turnover;
- new economically viable activities.

## 6. Value capture

Ask:

- Can the company charge for the gain?
- Does competition force the gain to customers?
- Does labor capture part of it?
- Does an upstream supplier capture it?
- Does the company own the customer relationship?
- Is the output differentiated?
- Is switching easy?

## 7. Feedback loop

Document exactly what becomes proprietary after execution.

Bad answer: "more data."

Better answer:

> Each executed workflow produces a labeled outcome tied to user context, which improves a specific future routing or product decision and cannot be reconstructed by a new entrant without replicating the usage history.

## 8. I-ROIC bridge

Start from operating evidence and bridge to economics.

    AI activity
      → measurable operational change
      → revenue / cost / asset-turnover change
      → incremental NOPAT
      → incremental invested capital
      → I-ROIC

## 9. Marginal return

Ask what happens to the **next** dollar of AI-related investment.

- More valuable sinks available?
- Same sink but more automation depth?
- Diminishing returns?
- Validation bottleneck?
- Power / compute bottleneck?
- Distribution bottleneck?
- Regulatory bottleneck?

## 10. Reinvestment runway

Estimate:

- number of unautomated / under-intelligent workflows;
- physical endpoint expansion;
- customer penetration;
- geographic expansion;
- new products made viable by cheaper intelligence;
- internal research leverage.

## 11. Valuation implication

Conclude with:

- base business value without major AI uplift;
- current measurable AI uplift;
- plausible future optionality;
- capital required;
- probability weighting;
- risk of value leakage;
- risk of overpaying for a true technology.

## 12. Disconfirming evidence

Every report must state what would make the thesis wrong.
