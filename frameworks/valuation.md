# Valuation Under I-ROIC

> Status: Early framework

## 1. Separate technology truth from price truth

Two statements can both be true:

1. AI causes a major productivity revolution.
2. Some AI-related securities are priced above the value they will ultimately capture.

Valuation must therefore estimate **captured cash flow**, not merely technological importance.

## 2. Value the sink, not the token alone

For an Intelligence Seller, external token/API economics may be directly observable.

For an Intelligence Allocator, much of the value may appear indirectly as:

- higher revenue;
- lower operating cost;
- faster product development;
- higher asset utilization;
- new products;
- lower working capital;
- improved retention;
- reduced failure rates.

The analyst must bridge these improvements to NOPAT and invested capital.

## 3. Internal consumption

Internal intelligence consumption should be evaluated against its opportunity cost.

If one unit of intelligence can be sold externally for X but generates more than X of risk-adjusted contribution internally, internal use can be rational.

This resembles vertical integration and capital allocation more than ordinary software pricing.

## 4. AI optionality

Do not capitalize every conceivable future sink.

Separate:

### Proven

Observed economics with repeatable evidence.

### Emerging

Economics visible in pilots or early cohorts but not mature.

### Optionality

Plausible future sinks with low current evidence.

Assign different probability and discounting to each.

## 5. Reinvestment matters more than one-time uplift

A company with 50% I-ROIC but almost no additional high-return sink capacity may be less valuable than a company with 25% marginal I-ROIC and a decade of runway.

The valuation question is:

> How much capital can be reinvested at attractive marginal I-ROIC, for how long?

## 6. Capture is the bridge to equity value

Gross social benefit is not shareholder value.

Track leakage to:

- customers;
- labor;
- model providers;
- compute providers;
- regulators / taxation;
- competitors.

Only retained economics belong in enterprise value.

## 7. Avoid terminal-value fantasy

AI narratives easily inflate terminal assumptions.

The terminal state should ask:

- Has intelligence become commodity-like?
- Is the sink still proprietary?
- Does feedback remain exclusive?
- What return survives after competition?
- What capital intensity remains?
- What replacement / maintenance burden exists?

## 8. Relationship to TRC

I-ROIC can help identify durable future earning power.

TRC or another investment strategy can separately determine whether the current market price offers enough expected return and margin of safety.

Do not merge business-quality analysis with entry-price discipline.
