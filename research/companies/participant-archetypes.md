# Participant Archetypes

> This file defines research categories, not investment conclusions.

## 1. Model-native companies

Research universe includes companies such as:

- OpenAI;
- Anthropic;
- DeepSeek;
- Kimi / Moonshot AI;
- other frontier or specialized model developers.

Core question:

> Is the highest-return use of their intelligence external token sales, internal production, or a mixture?

Research dimensions:

- model production cost;
- inference economics;
- distribution;
- internal research acceleration;
- product sinks;
- enterprise workflows;
- memory;
- agent execution;
- feedback loops;
- capital requirements.

## 2. Cloud and internet platforms

Examples for research can include Google, Microsoft, Meta and other large platforms.

Their models may be economically important even when token revenue is not the main profit pool.

Possible sinks:

- search;
- advertising;
- productivity software;
- cloud;
- social feeds;
- commerce;
- developer ecosystems;
- internal engineering.

Key question:

> Does AI strengthen an already-owned profit pool, create a new one, or merely raise the cost of defending the old one?

## 3. Open-source ecosystems

An open model can produce enormous user surplus without the originating organization capturing equivalent profit.

Research questions:

- who funds continued improvement?
- where does distribution live?
- which complements become scarce?
- do hosting, fine-tuning, enterprise support, data or orchestration capture the value?
- does openness commoditize the model layer and increase returns elsewhere?

## 4. Compute factories

This category includes GPU-rich infrastructure operators, hyperscalers and specialized compute providers.

Their economics should be analyzed as factories that transform:

    capital + chips + power + software
      → usable intelligence

Important metrics:

- utilization;
- power cost;
- depreciation;
- networking;
- tokens / useful tasks per dollar;
- customer concentration;
- switching;
- capacity cycles;
- residual value;
- software differentiation.

A compute factory with no high-return sink may earn commodity-like returns after scarcity fades.

## 5. Digital sink owners

These firms possess large digital workflows, distribution or transaction surfaces.

The relevant question is whether cheap intelligence can raise conversion, retention, product velocity, monetization, personalization or automation depth.

## 6. Physical sink owners

This is why Tesla and Xiaomi belong in I-ROIC research.

### Tesla research direction

Potential sinks to investigate include vehicles, autonomy, manufacturing, robotics, energy systems and internal engineering.

The question is not "Does Tesla have AI?" It is:

> How much valuable physical work can additional intelligence control, at what validation cost, and who captures the resulting surplus?

### Xiaomi research direction

Potential sinks to investigate include smartphones, vehicles, home devices, IoT, software surfaces, distribution and manufacturing.

The research question is:

> Does a broad human-car-home endpoint network create a large, proprietary, recurring sink for intelligence — and can Xiaomi retain the value rather than passing it through to consumers?

These are hypotheses to test with company-specific evidence.

## 7. Integrated intelligence allocators

The most important future category may be firms that combine:

- access to strong intelligence;
- proprietary sinks;
- action rights;
- feedback;
- capital allocation.

They may not be recognized primarily as "model companies."

I-ROIC research should actively search for them.
