# Personal I-ROIC

> Status: Working thesis
> Origin: 2026-10-06 extension of the company I-ROIC framework

## 1. Why the individual belongs in I-ROIC

If machine intelligence becomes cheap and abundant, an individual faces the same allocation problem as a company:

> Where should scarce attention, capital and machine intelligence be deployed so that the result compounds?

The risk is not only that an AI replaces a job.

A larger risk is that another individual with similar knowledge controls persistent memory, many agents, automated workflows, capital, distribution and owned products. Their effective productive capacity can become much larger than one human’s working hours.

## 2. Personal moat shifts downstream

Knowing how to prompt a frontier model is unlikely to be a durable moat.

Potential durable personal assets include:

- **Memory** — a machine-usable record of decisions, work, learning and context.
- **Capital** — financial resources that can be allocated.
- **Distribution** — an audience or channel through which useful work reaches people.
- **Relationships** — trust and repeated cooperation.
- **Owned Businesses** — products and systems whose output belongs to the individual.
- **Agent Organization** — persistent AI workers with goals, plans, memory and execution ability.

The model is the engine. These assets determine where the engine can create proprietary value.

## 3. From labor seller to system owner

A useful transition model is:

    Sell personal labor
      ↓
    Use AI to increase personal productivity
      ↓
    Delegate execution to persistent agents
      ↓
    Own products / businesses / distribution
      ↓
    Allocate capital and intelligence across assets

The end state is not "do no work." It is to move scarce human judgment toward allocation, goals, verification and irreversible decisions.

## 4. Personal Intelligence Sinks

An individual should distinguish between:

### Consumptive sink

AI helps complete a task, but little persists.

### Productive sink

AI output becomes an owned asset.

### Compounding sink

AI output becomes an asset that improves future AI output.

Examples of compounding residue:

- durable knowledge;
- reusable code;
- customer base;
- audience;
- capital;
- decision history;
- workflow memory;
- agent capability.

## 5. Practical experiment

A useful real-world test is to let a persistent AI organization manage several different owned assets rather than endlessly improving the organization in isolation.

Example asset classes:

- an AI-native product / business;
- a content and distribution portfolio;
- investment research and capital allocation.

Then perform a recurring allocation review:

> Which asset created the most durable value per unit of attention, capital and intelligence?

This turns Personal I-ROIC from philosophy into an operating experiment.

## 6. Five-year implication

Five years of ordinary AI usage may leave little moat.

Five years of compounding machine-readable memory, agent execution history, audience, owned business equity, investment capital, workflows and decision records can create a large path-dependent asset base.

That stock of assets may matter more than temporary access to the single best model.
